Citadel employs meteorologists, so the vibe is that weather forecasts must be the secret sauce. But a forecast only sees a week or two ahead, and a secret that comes free with your phone is not much of a secret.

I can't tell you what a firm with proprietary data and its own forecasters actually does. So I tested the version anyone can download: a free forecast, a laptop and unreasonable optimism.

The pipeline worked. The money didn't. Out of more than 12,000 weather-and-price pairs, hundreds looked like genius in the early years and turned into plain chance afterwards. Hundreds of thousands of weather-based trading rules later, nothing beat simply owning the market. Turns out the market reads the forecast too.

I did find one thing at the other end: rare, extreme events like a strong El Niño. The rarer the condition, the better it held up, but only inside my own method's selection rules, and a trading rule built on the rarest ones still lost money.

Hence the title: why everyday weather does not move markets, and where rare extremes might. The "might" is doing a lot of work. A live dashboard has been keeping score since 12 October.
