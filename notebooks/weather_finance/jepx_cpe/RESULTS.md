# ANSWER TO THE EXACT QUESTION (2026-10-12): standing on 20 August 2026, what would the model have said about September in the Tokyo area? (`answer_20aug2026.py`, `answer_20aug2026.csv`, `answer_20aug2026_figure.png`)
The perfect-foresight CPE below, applied with an 11-day lead (September starts 12 days after the 20th): baseline = mean price of the 30 days up to 20 August (known), window = 1-30 September, X = the window's mean ERA5 irradiance anomaly (perfect foresight, a ceiling for any real forecast), Y = ln(window mean price / baseline). Only windows that had finished by 19 August 2026 are used to estimate anything.
- The state: September 2026 as a 30-day window is at -0.63 daily-sd, rarer than 99% of 30-day windows in 1996-2015, so it sits beyond the 2% dull tail (cut -0.54).
- What history up to 19 August 2026 says, Tokyo all-day: dullest 5% of windows: 129 decision days, 9 episodes, P(price over the window in its top decile) = 0.00 against a base of 0.11, mean surprise -2.2 yen/kWh (22% of windows above the seasonal drift); dullest 2%: 73 days, 9 episodes, 0.00 against 0.12, -2.5 yen/kWh (14% up). Midday: dullest 5%: 0.00 against 0.15, -1.8 yen/kWh. The model would not have called a higher price.
- What happened: September 2026 averaged 23.78 yen/kWh all-day (24.31 midday), up 2.27 (3.53 midday) on the 21.51 (20.79) baseline; against the seasonal drift for August start dates (-0.73, -1.70) that is a surprise of +3.0 (+5.2). The model's sign was wrong.
- Why the history could not help: all nine episodes at that rarity are from 2016-2021 (the last begins in August 2021), the years when the price response to irradiance was weak and price levels low (baselines 6-11 yen/kWh against 21 now); there has been no dull window of that rarity since 2021 until September 2026. September 2026 is the first extreme in the high-price era, so the model had no comparable evidence, and the 30-day average washes out the daily effect found in the same-day CPE.
- Kyushu: September 2026 was not dull there (-0.21 daily-sd), so the same logic gives no signal.

# HEADLINE (2026-10-12): the perfect-foresight CPE (`cpe_oracle_forward.py`, `cpe_oracle_forward_yen.py`)
The question as asked: if we knew today that the next f days will be extreme in irradiance (ERA5 stands in for a perfect forecast, so this is a ceiling for any real one), is the midday JEPX price over those days an extreme move from today's level, and does trading it make money? Baseline = mean midday price of the 30 days up to the decision day (known then); outcome = mean price of the next f days; X thresholds from 1996-2015; Y thresholds frozen on 2016-05..2020-12; test start dates 2021-01..2026-08; season-matched base rates; money in yen/kWh against a causal seasonal drift; no costs; windows overlap (n_eff = n/f).
- Kyushu, 7-day windows, dull (dullest 10% of windows): CPE 0.53 against base 0.25 (lift 2.15, 33 episodes); the dullest 2% 0.54 against 0.22 (lift 2.5, 10 episodes). Lift rises with rarity at 7 and 14 days. Long on dull windows earned +3.1 yen/kWh per week (hit rate 90%, 126 start dates, about 18 independent weeks); 14 days +1.6 (86%, about 7 independent windows). The sign holds in both halves of the test window (+3.9 in 2021-01..2023-06, +2.7 in 2023-07..2026-08), against +0.5 (hit 58%) in 2018-2020.
- Kyushu, bright windows (short): +1.35 yen/kWh (68%) over the test window but +3.0 in 2021-23 and +0.6 after: not stable.
- Tokyo and the system price: nothing stable (7-day dull +0.2 yen/kWh, bright -0.3; sub-period results change sign). 30-day windows have fewer than 3 independent dull episodes and cannot be tested.
- On formation (2016-2020) Kyushu dull windows show lift 0.96, so the effect is not admitted by formation; it appears in the test years, when prices were higher.
- Reading: with perfect knowledge of the coming week, a dull-week signal pays in Kyushu midday in 2021-2026 and not elsewhere. It is a ceiling. The entry price here is a trailing spot average, not a forward price, so it is not known whether anything is left once the market has used the same weather forecasts. Earlier sections below are the intermediate tests this one replaces as the answer.

# CPE on irradiance extremes and JEPX prices (2026-10-12)

Script `jepx_cpe_scan.py`, table `jepx_cpe_scan.csv`, figure `jepx_cpe_figure.png`. The CPE definition and the program's gates, applied directly (not the full engine with its economic-prior registry).

Design, frozen before looking at JEPX. X: mean daily ERA5 irradiance z-score over the past p = 7, 14, 30, 60 days (z against the calendar-month mean and sd of 1996-2015, so the extreme thresholds come from before JEPX exists); both tails at 2%, 5%, 10%; Tokyo irradiance for Tokyo and the system price, Fukuoka for Kyushu. Y: next f = 7, 14, 30 days, (a) log change of the mean price against the previous f days, (b) mean premium over the system price; upper and lower 10% tails frozen on the formation window 2016-04..2020-12; test 2021-01..2026-09. Base rate matched to the same calendar months. 720 conditions.

Results
- Gate (CPE >= 0.80, lift >= 1.5, n >= 100 days): 0 of 720 pass on formation. 0 survivors.
- Looser, lift-only admission on formation (lift >= 1.5, n >= 100, >= 3 episodes): 73 conditions. In the test window their mean lift is 1.09 (median 1.09); 15% keep lift >= 1.5; 55% are above base.
- Average excess over the season-matched base in the test window (points): mechanism direction (dull -> price up, bright -> price down) -1.3 over 198 conditions; opposite direction -0.6. Dull states in the mechanism direction average -4.7.
- Only 7 conditions have lift >= 1.5 in both windows. Four run against the mechanism (dull -> Kyushu price down, bright -> Tokyo/system price up), and the others are lift 1.5-2 on 14-30 day windows with base rates of 4-15%.
- The one faint mechanism-consistent pocket is the Kyushu premium over the system price after bright spells (test excess +4.6 points over 30 conditions), but it is -1.7 on formation, the test base rate itself is about 0.5 because the Kyushu discount deepened after 2020, and it is not confirmed.

The 20 August 2026 state: over the past 30 and 60 days Tokyo irradiance stood at the 99th percentile of the 1996-2015 distribution, and Kyushu at the 100th (30-day state +0.88 sd against a bright-10% cut of +0.34). That is a bright extreme and in the mechanism direction implies a lower September price. The matching conditions (Tokyo, bright 10%, p = 30, f = 30) had formation lift 2.0 (CPE 0.25 against base 0.13) and test lift 0.93 (0.04 against 0.05). The outcome was the opposite: the dullest September since 1996 and a price of 23.8 against 20.1 in August.
Earlier note: the 80th percentile quoted in the first reply was for 1-19 August only.

Limits: ten years of prices; the windows overlap and conditions are highly correlated, so 720 rows are far fewer independent findings; no seasonal irradiance forecast; ERA5 grid cells.

## Midday-only rerun (slots 21-30, 10:00-15:00 JST; `python jepx_cpe_scan.py midday`, outputs `*_midday.*`)
Same 720 conditions, same frozen thresholds, targets built from the mean of the ten midday slots.
- Gate: 0 of 720 pass; 0 survivors. Lift-only admission: 82 conditions; test lift mean 1.04, 17% keep lift >= 1.5.
- Test excess over season-matched base: mechanism direction -4.1 points (198 conditions), opposite direction +4.2. The sign is reversed: dull spells are followed by LOWER midday prices, bright spells by HIGHER ones. Fourteen conditions hold lift >= 1.5 in both windows, all short (p = 7), with 30-49 episodes each, e.g. Kyushu dull 7 days -> price falls over the next 7 days: CPE 0.59 against base 0.29 in test.
- Diagnosis (`midday_contemporaneous_check.py`): the weather effect is real but contemporaneous. Same-week midday price against same-week irradiance anomaly: Kyushu -0.34, Tokyo -0.10. The 'move' target measures the next f days against the previous f days, and with p = f the previous days are the dull or bright days themselves, so the effect reverses the following week (correlation of this week's irradiance anomaly with next week's change +0.31 Kyushu, +0.19 Tokyo). Measured against the week before the weather (next week vs previous week) it is +0.09 for both: nothing left to forecast.
- Reading: irradiance moves the midday price in the same week, mainly in Kyushu; it does not predict the price a week or a month later, which is why nothing passes a forward-looking gate. The 20 August 2026 conclusion is unchanged.

## Change-in-irradiance rerun (`python jepx_cpe_scan.py diff [midday]`, outputs `*_diff*.*`)
X as the engine builds it for climate series: the change over the past p days, i.e. the mean anomaly of the last p days minus the mean anomaly of the p days before (thresholds from the 1996-2015 distribution of that change; "dull" = large fall, "bright" = large rise). Everything else unchanged.
- Full day: 0 of 720 pass the gate; 63 pass lift-only admission, test lift mean 1.24 (29% keep >= 1.5); mechanism direction -2.4 points, opposite -0.4.
- Midday: 0 of 720 pass; 60 admitted, test lift mean 1.12; mechanism direction -4.8, opposite +1.8; the 13 conditions with lift >= 1.5 in both windows are short (p = 7-14) and again run against the mechanism, with 15-70 episodes each.
- Same reading as the level version: the short-window reversal comes from the price-move target comparing the next f days with the previous f days (see the midday section), not from a forecastable signal.

## Would foreknowledge of a month's irradiance make money? (`oracle_money.py`, `oracle_money_months.csv`, `oracle_money_figure.png`)
Perfect-foresight upper bound with only irradiance and JEPX: for each month M from 2019-05 to 2026-09 (89 months; the first month with two earlier years of the same calendar month), reference R = mean price over days 1-19 of the previous month, outcome O = mean price over M, surprise = (O - R) minus the average (O - R) for the same calendar month in earlier years. Signal = ERA5 irradiance anomaly of M in monthly sd against 1996-2015; long 1 kWh if dull (bottom 10% cut), short if bright (top 10% cut), flat otherwise.
- The sign is right but the strength is small. Slope of the surprise on the anomaly (yen/kWh per sd, excluding Jan 2021 as the reference): Kyushu midday -0.83, Tokyo midday -0.22, system -0.04. Spearman correlations: Kyushu midday -0.22, Tokyo -0.01, system +0.07. Standard deviation of the surprise: 4.8, 6.7, 5.6 yen/kWh.
- Trades (excluding Feb 2021): Tokyo midday 30 trades, 50% hit rate, mean -0.39 yen/kWh (t -0.5). Kyushu midday 23 trades, 61% hit rate, mean +1.11 (t 1.6; Kyushu dull months 4 of 7 right, bright 10 of 16). With Jan 2021 included, mean P&L looks positive (+1.5 to +4.3) only because of a single month (a winter price spike, not weather).
- The ten most extreme months in Tokyo midday go the right way 4 times out of 10; in Kyushu 6 of 10.
- September 2026 itself (Tokyo irradiance -3.6 monthly sd, the lowest in the data): long would have gained +7.9 yen/kWh midday (+5.2 all-day) against the 1-19 August level after the seasonal drift. The other three dull Tokyo months lost in midday (Jan 2020, Jul 2020, Mar 2025).
- Reading: knowing a record-dull month in advance does not reliably make money in Tokyo and is a marginal, unproven edge in Kyushu midday; ten years of data.

### Checks and the oracle caveat (added)
Perfect foresight is an upper bound that no forecast can reach: ERA5 is a reanalysis, an after-the-fact estimate of what happened, so any real forecast of a month's irradiance does no better than the oracle result.
`oracle_money_checks.py` (independent code path) reproduces the oracle table exactly (Tokyo midday 30 trades, hit 0.50, mean -0.39; Kyushu midday 23 trades, hit 0.61, mean +1.11; September 2026 Tokyo midday +7.91 yen/kWh) and checks robustness:
- Data: JEPX columns checked against the header (system, Tokyo = column 8, Kyushu = column 14), every day has 48 slots, 2016-04-01 to 2026-10-13, no duplicates; ERA5 has no missing days, both sites in Asia/Tokyo time; slots 21-30 = 10:00-15:00 JST.
- Reference price: whole previous month instead of days 1-19: Tokyo mean -0.51 (t -0.8), Kyushu +0.91 (t 1.6).
- Cuts: 15%/85%: Tokyo 40 trades, hit 0.52, mean +0.60 (t 0.5); Kyushu 30 trades, hit 0.67, mean +1.26 (t 2.2). 20%/80%: Tokyo mean +0.30 (t 0.3); Kyushu +0.81 (t 1.5). The 10% cut was the one fixed in advance.
- Relative (log) version: Tokyo t 0.2, system t 0.05, Kyushu t 1.9 (18 trades, hit 0.61).
- Chronological halves: Tokyo 2019-05..2022-12 slope +0.15 (wrong sign), 11 trades, hit 0.27; 2023-01..2026-09 slope -0.44, 19 trades, hit 0.63. Kyushu keeps the sign in both halves (slope -1.15 and -0.61, hit 0.56 and 0.64, mean +1.23 and +1.04).
- Month price level against irradiance with no reference price at all, each year's average removed: Tokyo correlation -0.02, Kyushu -0.08.
Reading: Tokyo shows nothing under any specification; Kyushu midday shows a weak, consistent-sign effect (t 1.5-2.2), not proven on ten years.

### Is September 2026 paying because it is the most extreme month? (checked)
- Tokyo midday, all 88 months by |anomaly|: no gradient. Direction-adjusted P&L (long dull, short bright): |z| 1.5-2: +2.0 (hit 89%, 9 months); 2-2.5: -2.8 (hit 20%, 5 months); above 2.5: -1.3 (hit 40%, 10 months, mostly bright months, which do not pay).
- Dull side beyond -2 sd: Sep 2026 (-3.6) paid +7.9 yen/kWh; Jan 2020 (-2.7) -1.0; Jul 2020 (-2.0) -5.7.
- The only other month beyond -3 sd is September 2016 (-3.05, outside the scan because it has no two earlier years). Against 1-19 August, Tokyo midday moved -0.96 yen/kWh (-7%), the other nine Septembers a median -2.9 (-24%), 2026 +5.2 (+27%). Right direction in 2016, but about 1.7 yen better than usual against about 8 in 2026; fifth highest of the ten non-2026 Septembers.
- Two comparable events, one strong and one weak. In 2026 the price level was also high (19-24 yen against 10-15 in most Septembers), so lost solar displaces costlier fuel; that is a possible reason for the size and is untested.

## Does the magnitude of dullness matter? (`dose_response.py`, `dose_response_figure.png`)
Every non-holiday weekday 2016-04..2026-09 (2,583 days): midday price minus the mean midday price of the other such days within +/-15 days, grouped by the day's irradiance anomaly (ERA5, z against the calendar-month climate of 1996-2015).
- Kyushu: monotone and roughly proportional. Price response (yen/kWh) from dull to bright: +4.2 (< -2 sd), +3.8, +2.9, +1.8, +0.2 (normal), -1.7, -2.4, -3.4 (> +1.5 sd). The straight-line fit on the mild range (|z| < 1, -2.4 yen per sd) predicts +4.8 for the dull tail (z < -1.5) and +4.0 is observed.
- Tokyo: convex on the dull side. +5.4 (< -2 sd), +4.5, +1.5, +0.6, then about -0.8 to -1.0 on normal and bright days, flat. The mild range fit (-1.0 yen per sd) predicts +1.5 for the dull tail; +4.7 is observed, about three times larger. Bright days do not push the price down further.
- Stability: the response is about four times larger in 2021-2026 than in 2016-2020 in every bin (Tokyo < -2 sd: +9.0 against +1.2; Kyushu: +7.7 against +2.0), when price levels were higher.
- Season: Kyushu is positive for dull days in all four seasons (extreme-dull means +2.3 to +6.2). Tokyo is positive in December-February, March-May and October-November (+4.5 to +7.4, medians +2.0 to +4.1) but zero in June-September (-0.1): on a dull summer day lower air-conditioning demand appears to offset lost solar. Dull days are also cold or wet days, so some of the response is demand and not only lost solar.
- Same-day effect: the 7-day versions show little, with only 5 Tokyo windows beyond -1.5 sd, so this is a statement about individual days; with the baseline built from the surrounding weeks it cannot be read as a persistent effect.
- Limits: days inside a spell are not independent (188 distinct weeks behind 246 dull-tail days in Tokyo); the effect is contemporaneous, and the day-ahead price already uses the forecast for the day.

## The dose-response in the CPE framework: same-day CPE with a rarity gradient (`cpe_daily_dose.py`, `cpe_daily_dose_checks.py`, `cpe_daily_dose.csv`, `cpe_daily_dose_figure.png`)
CPE(q_X) = P(midday price response in its top decile | day's irradiance in the q_X dull tail), and the mirror for bright days (bottom decile). Y thresholds frozen on 2016-2020; test 2021-2026; base rate season matched; mechanism registered (dull -> up, bright -> down). Contemporaneous (tau_f = 0); ERA5 stands in for the sky; day-ahead prices already use the forecast. Admission adapted to a daily same-day effect (lift >= 1.5, >= 20 days, >= 10 episodes on formation); under the program's own gate (CPE >= 0.80, n >= 100) nothing passes, the highest test CPE being 0.70.
- Dull days, test CPE (lift over base) by rarity 20%, 10%, 5%, 2% of days: Tokyo 0.40 (3.0), 0.48 (3.8), 0.45 (4.1), 0.40 (4.7; 20 days); Kyushu 0.62 (2.6), 0.70 (3.0), 0.67 (3.2), 0.68 (3.6); system 0.49 (2.4), 0.59 (3.1), 0.57 (3.4), 0.55 (4.4). Base rates 0.09-0.24. Lift rises with rarity in every area in the test window and in Kyushu on formation too (2.6, 3.1, 3.1, 3.2); Tokyo formation 2.1, 2.7, 3.2, 1.1 (15 days at 2%).
- Admission: 18 of 24 mechanism rows pass on formation; all 18 have test lift >= 1.5 and are above base (mean test lift 2.65). The opposite direction (dull -> price in its bottom decile) has test lift 0.14-0.62.
- Bright days: Kyushu -> bottom decile lift 2.0-2.4 (admitted); Tokyo 1.6-1.8, not admitted (formation weak).
- Season (Tokyo, test): October-May dull days CPE 0.55-0.57 against base 0.12-0.14 (lift 4.2-4.7); June-September weak (lift 2.2 at 10%, 1.1 at 5%, 11-25 days), consistent with lower cooling demand offsetting lost solar. The season split was suggested by the dose-response and is not a pre-registered cut.
- Temperature control (Kyushu, test): with the day's temperature within 0.75 sd of normal the lift stays at 2.5, 2.9, 3.2 for the 20%, 10%, 5% tails (CPE 0.59, 0.74, 0.74 against 0.24, 0.25, 0.23), so the response is not simply cold-weather demand.
- Reading: the magnitude of dullness matters, same day, out of sample. It is a risk measure (how likely an extreme midday price on a dull day), not a forecast: nothing in it is known a month ahead.

### Does a dull day change the price on the following days? (`cpe_daily_lags.py`, clean baseline)
Same frozen CPE, price outcome k working days after the dull day (test 2021-2026). Lift at k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 14: Tokyo 10% tail 3.81, 1.01, 0.60, 0.85, 0.74, 0.72, 0.86; Tokyo 5% tail 4.12, 1.37, 0.87, 0.74, 1.13, 0.16, 0.87; Kyushu 10% tail 2.98, 1.20, 0.84, 1.14, 0.96, 0.99, 1.05; Kyushu 5% tail 3.24, 1.37, 1.07, 0.77, 0.88, 1.05, 0.80. The effect is gone from the next working day on; the small lift at k = 1 matches the chance that the next day is also dull (12-18% against 10%).
### What is and is not established (added)
- The design of the daily CPE (midday, percent response against +/-15 weekdays, mechanism direction) was chosen after the full-sample dose-response, which included the test years; the thresholds are frozen on 2016-2020 and the scoring is on 2021-2026, and Kyushu and Tokyo dull days show the effect on the formation window alone (lift 2.1-3.2), but the design itself was not fixed in advance.
- That the day-ahead price already carries the weather is an inference from the market design (day-ahead auction on the day before), not tested with forecast data.
- Small cells: Tokyo 2% tail has 15 formation and 20 test days; the Tokyo summer exception is post hoc.
