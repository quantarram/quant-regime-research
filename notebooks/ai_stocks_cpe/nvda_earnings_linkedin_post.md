Everyone knows the NVIDIA story: it reports, it beats, the stock pops, repeat. I pulled all 71 of its quarterly earnings reports since 2006 and checked that against the actual record, measured against the market and its own semiconductor peers.

There's no reliable pop. NVIDIA beats the S&P 500, AMD, and the semiconductor sector on only 44% to 55% of its earnings reactions, depending on the window — close to a coin flip. It even underperforms the market on the reaction day itself more often than not.

The part that actually surprised me: even through the 2023-2026 AI earnings supercycle, NVIDIA's one-month return *relative to its own sector peers* around earnings hasn't improved — if anything it's gotten worse (-3.65% vs. AMD, -2.25% vs. SOXX, compared with -1.20% and -0.29% before 2023). The enormous run hasn't come from the market rewarding its earnings days more than its peers' earnings days. It's come from a sustained trend between reports, not a repeatable earnings-reaction edge.

![NVIDIA's real excess return per earnings event, vs. market and sector peers, 2006-2026](nvda_earnings_study/nvda_earnings_excess_return_plot.png)

I separately checked whether the AI theme itself creates more tail-level co-movement across the stocks associated with it (a real, dated basket — Global X's AIQ ETF holdings exactly as they stood in December 2022, not picked with hindsight). It doesn't: real tail co-movement fell after ChatGPT's launch, and NVIDIA, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Apple each show zero surviving tail-level predictability from the rest of the basket in the post-launch window.

AI stocks are a real story at the level of the overall trend. The specific, tradeable patterns people assume come with that story don't show up when you actually check.
