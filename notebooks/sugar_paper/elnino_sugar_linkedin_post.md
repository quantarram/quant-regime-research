People love telling me "n=8 isn't a real sample size."

Fair point. There have only been eight El Niños since 1990. I can't exactly ask the planet for more of them.

Back in July, with India's monsoon deep in deficit under a confirmed El Niño, I made a call before knowing how it'd turn out: history said sugar prices spike over the next 1-3 months, 1.8x to 2x more often than not. Small sample, real pattern, flagged live.

The season just closed. Monsoon deficit did what the pattern said (-42% in June, narrowed to -12-15% by season's end). Sugar did what the pattern said (June's flag, three months forward, +29%). Every window that's closed so far is a hit.

I'm not going to pretend eight is a lot of events. It's not. But that's kind of the whole game with anything actually rare — hurricanes, crashes, super El Niños — you don't get thousands of them to average your way to comfort. You get a handful, and you find out fast whether your pattern only looks good in hindsight or actually holds up when tested live.

This one held. Historically it's right 71-81% of the time, not literally always — 1997 is the year it didn't work, and it's in the paper, not hidden. 2026 is the one I actually called in advance, and so far it's a clean hit.

Small samples aren't a bug when the thing you're studying is genuinely rare. That's just what a real one looks like up close.
