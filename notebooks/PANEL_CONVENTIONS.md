# Price-panel conventions (fixed 2026-10-13 to 21 in the live pipeline; archival research scripts unchanged)

Two properties of the stored panels (`multiasset_prices.parquet`, `multiasset_prices_live_history.parquet`) matter for any new analysis:

1. **FX bars (`...=X`) are dated one bar late from 2010-08-03 on** (Yahoo's dating; same-bar correlation of EURUSD with UUP is about 0, next-bar about -0.9). Align with `fx_align.realign_fx(prices)` (or `apply_if_needed`). The frozen file is left byte-identical so published results reproduce; the live history was converted once and the builders align every fresh download.
2. **The index is a union calendar: weekdays to 2013, every calendar day from 2014** (crypto trades daily; equities, ETFs, FX and futures are NaN on weekends). Shifting that frame by `tau` rows therefore means `tau` trading days before 2014 but `tau` calendar days after. Compute horizons on each instrument's own bars with `horizon_calendar.own_increment(prices, ticker, tau)` / `own_forward(...)`.

## Where the conventions are applied (live pipeline, all verified)
CPE pairwise and joint engines, `cpe_signal_ledger.py`, `cpe_signal_score.py`, the gold/portfolio/metals builders (aligned FX in memory and in every fresh download), `predictor_ledger.py` (EURUSD scored from the true as-of bar), `log_predictions.py` (scoring in trading days on one instrument), the climate research pipeline (`weather_finance/cpe_climate_run.py` and the four data builders) and the weather dashboard repo (`cpe_climate.py`, `climate_decisions.py`). The predictor_v1 live caches keep EURUSD on Yahoo's own dates by design (see the note in `predictor_v1/live_data.py`: aligning changes its features by under 3%).

## Archival research scripts (NOT changed)
`PANEL_CONVENTIONS_archival_scripts.txt` lists 173 scripts that read the panel with the old conventions (80 in `predictor_v1/`, 25 in `prior/`, 22 in `weather_finance/` outside the climate pipeline, 19 in the notebooks root such as the TSMOM/XSMOM studies, 16 in `files/`, and a few others). They produced the published papers and are kept as they were so those results reproduce. Their figures use row-based horizons on the union panel and unaligned FX; anything built on them for new work should load the panel through the two helpers above. The only effects on a given result are (a) FX targets or predictors shifted by one bar and (b) horizons after 2014 counted in calendar days instead of trading days.

## Gold / silver / platinum current state (fixed 2026-10-29)
`build_gold_dashboard.py` and `build_metals_dashboard.py` clip history to the frozen panel's last date (2026-06-16) for the historical distributions, and until 2026-10-29 also took the *current* 21/63/126/252-day changes, 52-week peak, drawdown, chart and (gold) the SGD rate from that clipped series, so "63d change" meant today's price against the price 63 bars before 16 June. The current state now comes from the live series; the distributions stay on the frozen panel. Logged gold/silver/platinum calls from 2026-06-18 to 2026-10-28 were restated by replaying every day point-in-time (`DASH_ASOF=YYYY-MM-DD` runs a builder offline from `multiasset_prices_live_history.parquet`, bars strictly before that date; see `asof_replay.py`). The as-logged files and the 2026-10-29 pages as published are kept in `legacy_stale_current_state/`. Replay of the old code on 2026-10-28/29 reproduces the logged silver and platinum scores exactly, so the change is the fix, not the replay. Row-level differences from the replay: the restated rows use the current frozen CPE tables and fully aligned live data, and the three weekend days 2026-08-29 to 08-31 had a GLD-derived price one trading day stale in the original log (kept as logged in `gcf_price_usd`).

The live price history also carried two spurious bars on futures and DXY (2026-09-07, US Labor Day, and 2026-09-20, a Sunday) that Yahoo no longer returns; the builders now drop stored non-FX bars after the panel's end that a fresh download no longer reports (`asof_replay.prune_unreported`), which shifts a few own-bar counts (CPE ledger rows resolved 4,465 to 4,448; portfolio signals firing 950 to 945 on 2026-10-29, tilts unchanged).

## One drawdown rule on all three pages (2026-10-29)
The drawdown component of the composite (35% gold, 40% silver/platinum) was a straight line on the 63-day change (-20% = 100, 0% = 0) on the gold page but a percentile rank of that change on the metals page, so a flat 63 days scored 70 on one and 0 on the other. Both now use the straight line. A check on the stored history (rank correlation of each rule with the next 21/63/126-day return, GC=F and SI=F from 2000, PPLT from 2010, panel to 2026-06-16) found no meaningful predictive power for either (between -0.07 and +0.14), the straight line equal or slightly higher for each metal. Separately, the gold page compared a simple % change with distributions of log returns when computing the 63/126-day percentile and the recovery table; the threshold is now converted to a log return first. Calls from 2026-06-18 to 2026-10-28 were restated again under these rules (the as-logged files are unchanged in `legacy_stale_current_state/`). The restated rows are a replay of rules fixed after the period; the live out-of-sample record starts with the 2026-10-29 calls.

## Gold changes use one instrument at both ends (2026-10-29, audit)
The gold page computed its 21/63/126/252-day changes (and drawdown from the peak) with the GLD-derived price as the current level over past GC=F bars, a mismatch of about 0.8 points on the 63-day change on average (GLD-derived sits about 0.5% above the futures). The current level is now the last settled GC=F bar; the GLD-derived price remains the displayed spot and the SGD-per-gram price. Re-replaying all 132 days moved no direction (largest score change 5.3 points on 2026-06-18, 54.4 to 49.2); the restated files were rewritten a third time.
